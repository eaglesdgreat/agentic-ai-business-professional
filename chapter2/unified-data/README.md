# Why Unified Data Matters

Businesses don't run from a single file. Information lives in separate tools that each team chose for its own work. Sales tracks leads in one system, operations runs an inventory database, and customer service logs tickets somewhere else. Each tool is good at its job, but the data stays locked behind departmental walls. That separation is called a data silo.

Silos quietly block the questions that matter most. Suppose you want to know whether customers who buy a particular product file more support tickets. You can't answer that when your sales data and your ticket data never meet.

Data integration solves this by joining separate sources into one unified platform. Picture pulling books from libraries across a city onto a single shelf you can reach from your desk. Once everything sits together, you can ask questions that span the whole business.

There is a trap, though. Once you can connect sources, it's tempting to drag files together right away. Combining data you don't understand yet leads to broken numbers. The fix is to slow down for one step first: take stock of what you have before you connect anything.

Key terms
- **Data silo:** data trapped inside one tool or department, isolated from the rest of the business.
- **Data integration:** joining separate data sources into one unified platform so they can be analyzed together.
- **Data source inventory:** a structured catalog of what data you have, where it comes from, and what shape it's in.
